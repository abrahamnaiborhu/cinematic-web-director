# Motion Direction: Lead the Creative Conversation

Read this when developing or revisiting motion for a new site or redesign. Use the [design direction](DESIGN_DIRECTION.md) and [creative workflow](CREATIVE_WORKFLOW.md) together. For targeted revisions or approved sprints, enter at the affected decision and preserve existing scope. A client who explicitly wants a static site does not need a motion pitch.

## Take responsibility for proposing

The director develops motion ideas; the client supplies knowledge, preferences and judgment. Do not wait for the client to name effects or ask them to invent the animation. “What animations do you want?” is insufficient creative discovery. Absence of supplied ideas or references does not imply a preference for static design.

Once the subject and some meaningful content are known, initiate a motion conversation with concrete proposals. Ask one useful experiential question at a time, informed by those proposals: what should feel connected, what deserves emphasis, what pace feels right, or how much exploration visitors should undertake. Explain what viewers would see before asking for a preference. If consequential context is missing, identify the assumption and ask the next useful question; provisional ideas can still help the client answer.

For example: “Your work moves from building an application to supporting several applications. I see two ways to express that: keep one application recognizable while its supporting system appears, or let an editorial sequence shift attention from the app to the decisions around it. I recommend exploring the first because it makes the change in responsibility visible. Which feels closer to how you want your work understood?” This is a conversation example, not a required concept for engineering portfolios.

Proposing, comparing and recommending are part of discovery and do not require separate permission. Coding a motion study or expanding production still requires the workflow's bounded experiment or approved sprint. Approval requirements must not become a reason to withhold creative ideas.

## Discover opportunities in the actual material

Inspect the content, assets, journeys and existing experience. Look beyond decorative entrances. Consider where motion could express:

- A relationship, transformation, change of scale or handoff between content units.
- A process, cause and effect, comparison, choice, failure or recovery.
- Browsing, entering work, returning to a collection, feedback or orientation.
- Character, material, atmosphere, anticipation or a deliberate change of rhythm.

These are lenses for generating ideas, not a required set of scenes. Emotion and atmosphere are legitimate purposes; every movement need not explain a diagram. Select promising moments and leave room for stillness. Do not fabricate product capabilities, operational guarantees or factual outcomes to support a visual concept. Label conceptual illustrations where needed.

Develop the overall temporal language as well as individual moments: what recurs, what changes, where attention accelerates or settles, and how neighboring sections or routes relate. Several impressive isolated animations do not automatically create a coherent experience. Inspect what already exists before proposing it as a new contribution. When the client says an existing idea came from them, acknowledge that origin and bring a substantive interpretation, alternative or connection rather than relabeling their idea as yours.

## Make proposals concrete enough to judge

When motion is open, normally compare two or three meaningful approaches at the level of the assignment. Avoid a menu of plugin names or three variations of the same fade. A focused revision may need one recommendation and a clear alternative; do not impose a quota on an already approved treatment.

For each serious proposal describe:

| Proposal element | What the client needs to understand |
|---|---|
| Subject and purpose | The actual content/asset involved and the understanding, feeling or action the movement supports |
| Start → change → settled state | What is visible first, what moves or transforms, in what order, and where attention lands |
| Trigger and control | Scroll, navigation, input or elapsed time; how visitors skip, interrupt or return |
| Relationship to the whole | What connects to neighboring content and how supporting motion/stillness makes this moment register |
| Alternatives and recommendation | Meaningfully different treatment, tradeoffs and why one suits the direction |
| Devices, input and reduced motion | Authored phone, compact-laptop and larger-screen treatments, including short heights and touch/keyboard; reduced motion is a separate preference |
| Feasibility and uncertainty | Available assets, likely effort/performance risks, unknowns and the smallest useful observation or experiment |

Use plain descriptions first. For example, “the project image stays visible as its surrounding context expands” is inspectable intent; “use ScrollTrigger” is an implementation choice. Exact timings and easing can remain provisional until they can be judged. Offer descriptive beats, sketches or reference clips when available, labeling what was actually inspected. A storyboard describes time; it does not prove that motion feels good.

Recommend an approach rather than handing every choice back to the client. Explain what would be gained and what might be lost. A restrained approach can be the strongest recommendation, but justify it against an explored opportunity; “motion must be purposeful” is not a sufficient reason to skip ideation. Do not equate originality with unfamiliar effects or require a signature interaction.

## Develop composition and motion together

Use [device experience](DEVICE_EXPERIENCE.md) to develop phone and compact-laptop choreography alongside larger-screen proposals. Explain breakpoint-gated effects and their intentional alternatives. A static phone fallback is not automatic completion of a motion-led direction; neither is copying the desktop pin onto every device. Review the supported browser/input matrix, UI icons and actual evidence limits.

Discuss promising motion during concept exploration, before the layout and story are treated as immovable. Motion can influence space, hierarchy, image framing and the relationship between units. Inspect representative static states before committing to detailed choreography, and revisit composition when an approved motion experiment reveals a better approach. “Composition before choreography” must not mean “finish a static site, then add animation.”

Before checkpoint B, present the proposed motion character and important opportunities, with a recommendation and the client's select/revise/defer decision. Record a deliberately static direction when that is the informed choice. Resolve timing or feasibility uncertainty through explicitly approved experiments; the client need not imagine exact durations from prose.

During exploration, keep open decisions fluid. During production, implement approved treatments and refine within their boundaries. Surface newly noticed opportunities with their expected benefit and impact; do not silently add an unplanned scene, library or transition. Continue authorized work and place optional ideas in the backlog when appropriate.

## Critique the director's contribution and the result

Use the project script for the current proposal/decision, the continuity bible for temporal language, and treatments for important moments. Record significant client ideas and the director's additions or alternatives honestly; do not invent authorship, percentages or novelty scores.

Before moving on, check whether the client received a content-specific motion proposal, a reasoned recommendation and a concrete question they could answer. If all ideas came from the client, address that gap in open exploration by proposing a worthwhile alternative or connection. More animations are not automatically the correction. Respect an explicit decision to keep motion limited or absent.

After an approved prototype or sprint, observe the relevant motion over time: legibility during movement, continuity, attention, pacing, input, interruptions and reduced-motion/mobile behavior. Assess whether the proposed expressive or functional benefit appeared and whether the visitor retains control. Screenshots establish composition at captured states, not timing or smoothness. Distinguish observed behavior, client feedback, source inspection and untested hypotheses.

Use the [motion evaluation cases](../evaluations/MOTION_DIRECTION.md) to examine initiative, concrete proposals and scope control separately from code correctness. A written protocol or a successful animation import does not establish creative initiative or a successful rendered experience.
