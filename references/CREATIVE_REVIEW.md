# Creative review: from proposal to inspectable evidence

Use when a client is choosing a direction, reviewing a composition or judging an implemented slice. Read only the relevant parts. Reuse the project's existing review documents; [the review package](../templates/REVIEW_PACKAGE.md) is an adaptable structure. Keep a concise recommendation in the conversation and link the detailed evidence. Do not make the client assemble a decision from several long technical documents.

## Make the next decision concrete

State the decision, the recommended choice, its visitor benefit and what remains uncertain. Develop the preferred candidate rather than repeatedly reopening the whole concept contest. A preference such as “leaning toward it” does not establish visual approval; identify the material/version the client has actually judged. Existing scoped approvals remain valid.

Match evidence to the decision:

| Decision | Useful evidence | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Content framing and hierarchy | Actual draft copy, supplied images, annotated layout/sketch and key visitor route | Pixel fit, visual polish or observed motion |
| Visual composition | Rendered representative entry and substantive content, including phone and compact/short laptop views | Timing, interaction reliability or other browsers |
| Temporal character or feasibility | Start/change/settled storyboard, then an approved interactive study or recording when observation is needed | A storyboard alone does not demonstrate feel; a recording does not prove controls work |
| Production acceptance | Actual journeys and relevant runtime conditions, linked observations and client response | Untested platforms or visitor understanding inferred from code |

Include a meaningful content state beyond the opening when judging a site direction. A portfolio needs to reveal work and its context; a product site needs an evaluation path; a landing page needs enough explanation to support the next action. Choose representative cases, not a compulsory screenshot count. Show the same substantive content in the important small-screen treatments; shrinking a desktop hero does not demonstrate the experience.

Document sketches are useful early evidence. If rendering is unavailable or coding is unapproved, provide the best permitted review board, label its limits and propose the smallest step needed for the next decision. Do not claim a finished visual review or block all discussion until a browser exists. A static comp is not permission to code choreography; apply the existing scoped prototype/sprint agreement where needed.

For movement, show what the visitor sees first, what changes, where attention should land and what remains accessible. Compare movement with the settled still composition when its benefit is uncertain. Give the client a concrete viewing prompt and a real choice to adopt, revise or reject. Avoid teaching easing/plugin terminology when a visible comparison answers the question.

## Direct the imagery, not just its placement

Inspect the actual available assets and explain what each contributes to the story. Record source/status and proposed treatment separately: a supplied file is not necessarily usable, approved, licensed for publication or representative of current work. Verify relevant provenance/usage rights where unclear without imposing a separate approval ritual on already authorized assets.

For consequential imagery decide: subject and role, selection/sequence, crop/scale, background/contrast, connection to copy and suitability for phones. Preserve details needed to understand the work or product. A finished artwork does not establish a making process; a conceptual workflow is not a product screenshot; a generated workshop image is not documentary evidence.

Turn a gap into a specific next action. For example: request a product state showing owner assignment, commission a real workshop-detail photograph, or use an explicitly labeled diagram while evidence is unavailable. Explain whether that gap blocks the current decision or only later publication. Offer a suitable alternative that preserves factual honesty. Do not fill missing proof with invented clients, metrics, testimonials or synthetic documentation.

Use a compact asset plan when helpful: source/status → narrative role → treatment/phone adaptation → gap, owner and next action. Select or commission new imagery within the authorized scope; tool availability does not determine the art direction.

## Check what a visitor understands

Before assessing the work, define a few tasks grounded in the actual goal. Examples: explain what is offered and whether it is for you; identify the author's contribution in a case study; find pricing or documentation; locate a date and attendance requirements. Do not prescribe these examples to every site.

Let an actual newcomer attempt the task from representative material without giving them the intended answer. Capture their words/actions and where they looked, then compare them with the intended takeaway. A client review is valuable but may not represent a newcomer. If only an independent synthetic reviewer is available, label it as such: it can expose ambiguity, not establish human usability, conversion or comprehension rates. Use another agent only when delegation is available and authorized; otherwise record the missing visitor evidence and a practical next step.

Distinguish the intended answer, the director's prediction and an observed response. A checklist saying “the message is clear” is not a visitor test. Do not invent a success percentage from a handful of reviews. Revise a named ambiguity, then revisit the affected task rather than endlessly polishing every page.

## Turn runtime acceptance into observable cases

Derive cases from the treatment and agreed [device coverage](DEVICE_EXPERIENCE.md). For each relevant condition record the expected result, actual action/observation, artifact/build, environment, evidence and remaining limit. Keep planned checks separate from completed results.

Useful conditions include:

- First load and late/missing assets or scripts: important content/actions remain available; fallback layout still makes sense.
- During movement: text and important detail remain legible, the intended connection can be followed, and actions are not held hostage to an introduction.
- Interruption, repeat use and return: fast/reverse scrolling, navigation, Back, stop/replay or direct entry behave according to the treatment. Exercise the controls, not merely their presence in source.
- Resize, short height, text enlargement and changing input: content reflows, focus persists and a touch visitor has an equivalent route. Test only the conditions relevant to this slice; broader coverage remains recorded.
- Reduced motion: the actual alternate state is complete and usable, independently of touch/viewport adaptation.
- Performance where material: measure the actual interaction under stated device/load conditions. Define a proportionate budget from project needs; do not invent FPS or treat a local desktop trace as mobile field performance.

Screenshots establish visible states; a recording or interactive observation supports timing; interaction checks support behavior. Preserve enough evidence to inspect the claim and reproduce the scenario, using the tools available. Two PNGs passing preflight cannot replace this record. Label browser emulation, real hardware, client reports and source-only findings distinctly.

## Close the review and continue

Record the client's decision against a version, evidence that changed the direction, unresolved blockers and the next authorized work. Update existing script/treatment/iteration records by reference rather than maintaining conflicting specifications. Fix in-scope defects and continue approved sprints. Reopen only affected decisions when content, direction, scope or model authorization changes. Missing evidence is a named next step, not a claim that the entire experience has passed.
