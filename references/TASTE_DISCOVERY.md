# Make creative preferences concrete

Use during open visual/motion discovery when the client would benefit from examples, especially when they know development but not design vocabulary. Preserve their supplied brief, delegated choices and approvals. A client can skip this aid or describe a taste outside it; do not impose another interview on an approved sprint or bounded planning request.

## Offer a small, inspectable starting point

The bundled [taste guide](../assets/taste-guide.html) is a self-contained HTML file with eight original fictional static studies. Open it in a browser, provide its actual file link, or copy it to an agreed project location such as `creative/taste-guide.html` when useful for client review. Preserve existing files; do not overwrite a client's guide or reorganize their references. No installation, server, remote font or network connection is required to view the file. JavaScript enables local note generation; the studies and discussion prompts remain readable without it. Notes are not sent or saved by the page; the client copies them into the conversation before closing it.

The examples explore editorial hierarchy, raw/brutalist structure, object-led restraint, expressive typography, playful eclecticism, tactile illustration, technical clarity and experimental art direction. These are useful descriptions for this library, not an exhaustive taxonomy, authentic historical classifications or eight preset website templates. Different fictional subjects demonstrate possible qualities, not a rule that law firms, products or portfolios must use a certain style. No third-party client work or motion is represented as observed evidence.

Start with a few relevant or contrasting studies rather than requiring all eight. Invite the client to identify qualities they like, dislike or find uncertain: hierarchy, type scale, density, contrast, material, spacing, irregularity or image role. Ask why a quality matters for their visitors and subject. "The type of 04 with the clarity of 01, but neither palette" is a valid answer. A dislike or "none of these" is useful evidence; do not convert curiosity, silence or an unreviewed example into selection or approval.

Use a concrete question supported by the inspected example, then wait and follow up. For example, when a client likes the raw radio study, explore whether it is the bold type, exposed structure or deliberately rough character. Preserve the specific quality they want to borrow; do not import the entire composition, fictional story, palette or stack. The client need not know the label "brutalism" to make that judgment. If browser viewing is unavailable, explain the actual compositions verbally and label the limitation; do not claim the client saw them.

## Separate the choices that matter

Distinguish visual language from eccentricity, artistic expression and motion appetite. These do not form a single quiet-to-loud style score. A technical product can have maximal eccentricity, a gallery-like page can have expressive motion, and a raw visual language can use direct, still interactions. The guide's initial "uncertain" preferences are open decisions, not defaults to restraint.

Take "maximum" seriously. Develop subject-specific ambitious alternatives, explain consequences and seek review instead of silently returning to conventional layouts/fades. Maximum expression does not authorize arbitrary effects, illegible essential content or unlimited resource use. Use the [whole-experience plan](EXPERIENCE_SYSTEM.md) to solve visitor control, phone/compact-laptop treatment, access and performance alongside the creative ambition. Discuss a consequential conflict rather than quietly downgrading the agreed intent.

Static samples cannot establish animation feel, timing or choreography. Discuss motion separately using [motion direction](MOTION_DIRECTION.md): describe concrete start/change/settled states and compare movement roles, pace and visitor control. A client choosing expressive type has not selected a marquee, scroll lock or transition library. Coded motion studies keep their existing explicit bounded approval requirements.

## Carry preferences into the client's own direction

Record qualities and reasons, rejected qualities, separate ambition preferences, explicit delegation and unresolved tensions in the project script or existing equivalent. The guide's generated notes are provisional client input, not creative-direction or implementation approval. Do not infer consent to frameworks, migrations or new features from them.

Once discovery is ready, use the client's actual content to develop materially different concepts and inspect representative compositions. Explain how each responds to the expressed taste and where an adaptation is needed. Keep style, structure, copy and motion approvals distinct, and allow revision after viewing examples or an approved study. This aid improves the conversation; a selected label or generated summary cannot prove artistic quality, comprehension or runtime behavior.
