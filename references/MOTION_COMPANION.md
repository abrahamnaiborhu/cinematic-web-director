# Help the client judge a moving experience

Use during open discovery or motion exploration when concrete comparison helps. The [bundled companion](../assets/motion-companion.html) is an original fictional collection-to-project study with three authored relationships: keep the selected object recognizable; reorganize the collection into context; or cut into a different visual room. The cut is intentionally instantaneous. Each treatment can be viewed without interpolation; none is a required recipe for client websites.

The companion opens as a standalone local HTML file. It uses original vector illustrations, platform animation APIs and no external dependencies or remote requests. Open/link it or copy it to an agreed project location alongside `taste-guide.html` if the visual-guide backlink is wanted. Preserve existing files. The motion comparison itself works independently of that backlink. A server or package installation is not required; without JavaScript it retains native links to still case descriptions. It does not reproduce a client's artwork or demonstrate all browsers/devices.

## Use observed movement to ask better questions

Start from the actual brief and [taste preferences](TASTE_DISCOVERY.md). Choose relevant contrasts and explain what to watch: recognition, context, a change of world, attention, pacing or visitor agency. Let the client open, pause, finish, replay and return. Page load and treatment selection do not start motion. OS reduced-motion preference defaults to stillness; explicit playback remains optional. Explain the phone composition and the limits of available runtime evidence.

Ask what appealed and why. "B's rearrangement, with more reading time" or "none of these expresses this work" is more useful than a winning letter. A viewed demonstration can help articulate preference, but does not establish that the same choreography suits the client's subject. Do not reuse the fictional story, type/palette, trajectories or browser code automatically. A recommendation must identify the client's own content relationship, not merely a matching style label. Preserve maximum creative appetite rather than equating orientation/access with blandness.

Distinguish still frames, a plan, actual observed playback and client feedback. Do not claim to have watched the companion when you only read its source. If it cannot be viewed, offer clearly labeled verbal start/change/settled descriptions and retain timing/feel as uncertain. The generated notes are provisional reactions, not an approval record or proof that playback was watched.

## Make the interpretation inspectable

For a consequential creative proposal, make these connections clear in natural language; use the amount of structure the decision needs:

- **What I heard:** confirmed audience/content, specific liked/rejected qualities, ambition, constraints and delegation. Separate facts, preference and unknowns.
- **What I recommend:** a subject-specific direction, a meaningful alternative and the reason for the recommendation. Go beyond variations of one fade or easing curve.
- **What you would experience:** concrete subject and start/change/settled states, visitor trigger/control, important phone/compact-laptop adaptation and reduced-motion access. Include neighboring content or return behavior when consequential.
- **What remains uncertain:** missing assets, interpretation, timing, usability or technical cost, and the smallest next question, composition review or bounded experiment that can resolve it.

This is a reasoning aid, not a mandatory script for every reply. In early discovery, a short example plus the next consequential question may be enough. Do not turn it into a full concept pitch before hearing needed answers. A complete brief or explicit delegation should not trigger repeated interviews.

## Carry the conversation to the right boundary

Move from taste and motion reactions to project-specific concepts, representative composition review and a proposed scoped study. The bundled study is skill-maintenance material already built; playing it does not require approval to code a new client prototype. Writing a new coded composition/motion study still needs its explicit bounded agreement: question to resolve, permitted files/assets/dependencies, devices/input/reduced-motion checks, expected evidence, model scope, time/iteration boundary and stopping conditions. Direction approval or "I like B" does not supply that agreement.

Once an identified experiment or sprint is sufficiently specified and approved, execute continuously inside it; do not repeatedly request the same permission. Preserve approved constraints and use the [whole-experience plan](EXPERIENCE_SYSTEM.md) for loading, interruption/navigation, lifecycle and budgets. Source-only plans, an attractive demo and offline preflight results do not prove the actual client's runtime or creative outcome.
