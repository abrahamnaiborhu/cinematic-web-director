# Design Direction: From Subject to Experience

Read this when developing or substantially revising a creative direction, especially for an existing-site redesign. Use it with the [creative workflow](CREATIVE_WORKFLOW.md); its checkpoints, prototype permissions and model agreements still apply. Resume approved work at the relevant decision rather than reopening discovery automatically.

## Establish the freedom to redesign

Use the client's stated intent. If the degree of change is consequential and unclear, ask whether they want a focused refresh, a substantial redesign or a complete creative rethink. Explain these through outcomes rather than making the client choose terminology. A request to repair one interaction does not authorize reimagining the site.

For a creative redesign, derive the proposed experience from the subject, audience, content and intended visitor understanding. Evaluate the existing presentation; do not adopt it as the default composition. Conversely, do not discard something useful just to appear different. Preserve client-required functionality and approved constraints unless their change is authorized.

Inspect the old site and its implementation, then distinguish:

| Existing material | Design treatment |
|---|---|
| Facts, work samples, product information | Validate freshness and contribution; use as evidence for the new story |
| Required behavior, routes, integrations and accessibility needs | Preserve the requirement; explore how its presentation can change |
| Explicit brand/client constraints | Record their source and allowed flexibility; retain until a change is approved |
| Layout, section order, type, palette, imagery treatment and effects | Treat as candidates to retain, revise or replace with a stated reason |
| Reusable components or technical infrastructure | Evaluate implementation reuse separately from visual reuse |

Keep a concise record of consequential inherited decisions in the project script: what carries over, why, whether it is required or proposed, and its approval status. A screenshot, CSS token or reusable hero component does not establish a client requirement. Ask about ambiguous constraints before depending on them.

## Write the story without borrowing the old section list

Summarize the subject and visitor need in plain language before choosing a visual style. Establish:

- What visitors should understand, believe or be able to do.
- Which specific work, facts, demonstrations or artifacts support that understanding.
- What deserves prominence, what needs explanation and what can be removed or deferred.
- How visitors will encounter the content: browse, compare, read a case study, explore a demonstration or follow a guided sequence.

Use confirmed content, not invented achievements or generic adjectives. For a portfolio, distinguish the person's contribution from the team's output. Treat missing imagery, weak descriptions or unsupported claims as content problems to resolve; decorative motion does not supply missing evidence.

Build a content hierarchy and candidate journeys from this brief. Existing headings such as About, Skills and Projects can remain when useful, but their presence in the old site is not a reason to keep their order, prominence or format. Mark structure as provisional where exploration is allowed. Respect explicitly fixed or previously approved structure; propose and obtain agreement for changes outside its allowed flexibility.

A coherent story can be a curated body of work, an argument supported by evidence or several useful visitor routes. It need not be a linear scroll narrative. A central metaphor is one possible organizing device, alongside editorial framing, a collection, a process or a functional demonstration. Do not force every label, page or interaction into a metaphor at the expense of clarity.

## Explore different ways of presenting the subject

Use the workflow's [discovery readiness check](CREATIVE_WORKFLOW.md#1-intake-and-adaptive-discovery) before a full concept pitch. Clarify consequential visual preferences and motion experience alongside purpose/content; distinguish a small illustration used to ask a question from a developed direction. Explicit requests for immediate provisional ideas or delegated choices remain valid. Do not replace unanswered discovery with a polished concept document.

When the direction is open, normally offer two or three meaningful alternatives, scaling the exploration to the assignment. A client-selected or already approved direction does not need an artificial concept contest. Give each candidate:

- A specific organizing idea and intended visitor takeaway.
- A content hierarchy and composition that express that idea.
- Roles for actual work, imagery, typography, space and recurring elements.
- A browsing/interaction approach and a reason for any prominent movement.
- Representative compositions, important mobile changes, tradeoffs and unresolved assumptions.

Different names, palettes or fonts on the same hero and card layout do not establish different directions. Compare how candidates frame the evidence, arrange attention and support journeys. They may share justified navigation or other functional conventions. Explain differences through what a visitor sees and does, and recommend a candidate on fit rather than novelty alone.

Study references by quality: hierarchy, image treatment, transitions, atmosphere, material, density or pacing. Record what was actually inspected, what the client values and how the quality will be interpreted for this subject. Avoid copying a reference's whole composition. Familiar techniques are acceptable when well suited; unfamiliarity alone is not a quality criterion.

For example, if confirmed work supports the thesis “I make complex systems understandable,” an architect's portfolio might explore a relationship map that reveals how projects connect, or an editorial collection exposing decisions and tradeoffs in independent case studies. These are illustrative possibilities, not required metaphors, layouts or claims for engineering portfolios.

## Develop composition alongside motion intent

Use [motion direction](MOTION_DIRECTION.md) to initiate motion ideas during concept exploration. Consider relationships, temporal character and expressive possibilities while layouts are still provisional. Inspect static states before detailed choreography; do not postpone all motion thinking until a static design is finished.

Use representative real content to examine hierarchy, readable text, imagery, spacing, density, scale and navigation. Show enough of the entry, substantive content and smaller-screen experience to judge the proposed direction; a hero alone rarely establishes how case studies or product information will work. Adapt the views to the actual journeys rather than prescribing a fixed number of frames.

Follow [device experience](DEVICE_EXPERIENCE.md) to develop phone and compact-laptop compositions from the beginning, including limited viewport height. A scaled-down large-screen layout or a missing mobile motion sequence does not by itself establish an intentional responsive direction.

Use inspectable sketches, annotated layouts, mockups or other available visual tools. Identify the artifact, actual content and limitations. A textual description remains a proposal, not evidence that a composition looks good. If a coded composition study or motion experiment is needed, first agree on its bounded prototype scope under the workflow. Missing visual tools can permit discussion to continue, but do not waive representative-composition review before production.

Develop the visual language through these studies. Choose typography roles and reading rhythm, palette roles, imagery treatment, geometry and spatial relationships for this direction. Extreme type, asymmetry, restrained color or deliberate grid breaks are possible choices, not universal requirements. Body text must remain comfortable to read; display drama must not obscure necessary content or actions. A familiar font or a useful feature grid is not inherently a failure.

Develop concrete proposals for movement that contributes orientation, explanation, emphasis, atmosphere or feedback. A signature interaction may help a concept, but no fixed count is required. Give prominent moments room to register and supporting motion a proportionate role. Pinning, marquees, word reveals, smooth scrolling, 3D objects and fixed easing/duration recipes require a project-specific purpose; none is a default checklist. Recommend an approach and invite informed feedback; restraint must not substitute for ideation. Choose implementation tools after the intended behavior is clear.

Client-requested visual constraints remain valid inputs. Explain conflicts with content or usability and seek agreement on alternatives instead of silently ignoring them. Treat requests for award-level craft as an ambition; do not claim personal awards, clients or professional history.

## Critique, revise and converge

Review candidate compositions and the evolving rendered site using these questions:

| Question | Useful evidence or correction |
|---|---|
| Does the subject come through? | Identify actual work, content and visual decisions that express the intended understanding |
| Could this fit an unrelated subject after replacing the name? | Find interchangeable framing and develop more specific content/composition; shared conventions alone are not a defect |
| Is the old design still deciding the new one? | Explain consequential inherited choices against requirements and the selected direction; change unjustified carryover |
| Can visitors find, read and use the substance? | Inspect real content and journeys at representative sizes and with motion reduced; correct hierarchy or access problems |
| Does movement improve the experience? | Observe timing, interruptions and orientation; simplify effects that compete with the work or delay access |

Use these as qualitative prompts, not an invented originality score or a required percentage of visual change. Record the weakness, proposed adjustment and evidence in the iteration log. Compare candidates and revisions against the same intended visitor understanding.

During exploration, revise the thesis, structure and visual language when evidence warrants it, keeping provisional decisions visibly provisional. During an approved sprint, refine within the agreed boundaries; changes to approved intent, fixed structure or scope require the relevant decision. Continue unaffected authorized work. Converge on a coherent direction and agreed acceptance criteria instead of refining indefinitely.

Before creative-direction approval, summarize the chosen idea, supporting content, inspected compositions, consequential inheritance decisions, interaction emphasis and remaining uncertainty. Link them from the project script and continuity bible. The [evaluation scenarios](../evaluations/DESIGN_DIRECTION.md) support reviewing this skill's behavior; their existence does not establish that a live design trial passed.
