## Problem and intended improvement

The client reports bland animation and insufficient eccentricity. v0.5 demonstrates initiative and clearer review planning, but its bounded trials do not establish distinctive art direction. Its portfolio fixture explicitly favors a thoughtful character, its workshop favors patience/warmth, and its product emphasizes evaluation. These are useful cases, but weak evidence for adventurous creative range.

Test two possible weaknesses separately: conventional concepts, and generic execution of potentially interesting concepts. The next director should help clients choose a desired emotional character, originate an ambitious interpretation when appropriate, develop recognizable movement behavior and critique the actual result. Distinctiveness can also be quiet; an explicit calm or static brief remains authoritative.

## Recommended changes to test

- Establish creative ambition through understandable contrasts and examples. The director brings possibilities before asking for preferences; the client need not invent animations or know specialist terminology.
- Give a consequential adventurous interpretation serious consideration when the client seeks distinctiveness. Explain its subject connection, expressive promise, required assets and tradeoffs. Recommend on creative fit and craft alongside feasibility; implementation convenience does not decide by default.
- Develop a small, coherent set of movement behaviors for the chosen direction. Discuss anticipation, acceleration, pauses, following movement, deformation, spatial relationships and material response where useful. Explain what a visitor sees and feels. These are creative tools, not a required effect inventory or a universal easing recipe.
- Critique concept and execution separately: what feels specific, what remains interchangeable, where the timing loses character, and whether the proposed feeling appears in observed motion. Offer concrete revisions when something works technically but feels generic.
- Preserve the character in authored phone and compact-laptop treatments. Reduced motion remains a separate preference with a complete alternative. Device adaptations should retain the intended identity and content access.

Extend the existing motion/design guidance and relevant treatment fields only where the trials justify it. Keep the entrypoint concise; avoid another large mandatory questionnaire or a fixed restrained/expressive/experimental menu for every assignment.

## Judging the improvement

Inspect whether the director originates a surprising interpretation grounded in the project, develops coherent movement behavior and explains why it recommends the direction. Compare actual movement for emotional fit, rhythm, spatial/material credibility and memorable character. Identify where it resembles a generic entrance/transition with different content.

Keep creative judgment, visitor understanding and functional/device checks separate. A technical pass cannot answer whether an idea is compelling; unusual behavior alone cannot establish quality. Capture the client's reaction in their own terms and revise a named weakness rather than adding effects indiscriminately.

The release claim must match the evidence: discovery trials establish proposal behavior; an observed slice establishes only that slice; synthetic review does not establish human taste or usability; viewport emulation does not establish physical-device compatibility. Broader real-client use remains necessary.
